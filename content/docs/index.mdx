Two kinds of material, used differently.

**Concept pages** build the model. Each opens with a failure you can reproduce,
explains why the mechanism was designed that way, and ends with a lab you run and
self-check questions you can't answer from memory alone. Start here.

**Reference questions** test recall once the model is built. Question, model
answer, why it matters, follow-ups. Use them to self-test, not to learn from
cold.

## Working with it

- **Search covers collapsed answers.** `Ctrl` `K` searches the full text of every
  answer and follow-up, not just the visible headings.
- **Every question is a deep link.** `/docs/java/concurrency#q48` opens that one
  answer expanded and scrolls to it — useful for citing a specific claim.
- **Numbering restarts per bank**, so `#q1` exists three times. The path is what
  disambiguates: `/docs/java/core-language#q1` and
  `/docs/data/modelling-indexing#q1` are different questions.
- **19 diagrams** sit inside the answers they illustrate — the JVM memory areas,
  G1's mixed collection, MVCC tuple versions, Kafka's high watermark, the
  transactional outbox, hexagonal ports and adapters, and others.

## What's here, and what isn't

The reference conversion is **complete**: all 29 sections, 407 questions, 283
follow-ups, converted from `_source/` without rewriting a word of the answers.

The concept pages are the point of the library and are **barely started** — one
written of roughly twelve foundational candidates. The `leading/` track is empty
by design; those pages have to come from real experience rather than be
generated. And the study-path graph that `prerequisites`/`unlocks` is meant to
drive isn't built yet, so for now the sidebar is the map.
