title: Reviewing for Design, Not Style
description: What a senior reviewer looks for once formatting is automated and the code compiles.
tags: [leading, code-review, design]
---

What this essay should cover: what you actually read a diff for once style is a
tool's problem — which design problems are worth blocking a merge over, which
are worth a comment, and which you let through because the cost of being right
is higher than the cost of being wrong.

## Questions to answer

1. Walk through a review where you rejected code that worked. What was wrong
   with it, and how did you explain the cost to the author?
2. What is the first thing you look at in an unfamiliar diff — the tests, the
   interface, the call sites, the migration? Why that first?
3. Describe a comment you left that you later regretted. Was it wrong, or was
   it right and badly delivered?
4. Where is your line between how you would have written it and it being wrong?
   Give a case where you consciously stayed on the near side of that line.
5. Describe a design problem you missed in review that shipped and cost
   something. What would have caught it — a different reading order, a question
   you failed to ask, a test that did not exist?
6. How do you review a change from someone much more junior differently from one
   by a peer? What changes: the standard, the tone, or the number of comments?
