What this essay should cover: what you personally do from the moment a page
fires — how you split diagnosis from communication, when you stop investigating
and mitigate, and how the review afterwards avoids becoming either a blameless
ritual or a search for a culprit.

## Questions to answer

1. Walk through one incident end to end, from the first signal to the all-clear.
   Where did the time actually go?
2. When did you stop looking for the cause and start mitigating? What made you
   switch, and have you ever switched too late?
3. What did you say to people outside the incident, and how often? Who decided
   that — you, or a process?
4. Describe an incident where the first hypothesis was wrong and it cost you.
   What would have made you drop it sooner?
5. Name an action item from a post-incident review that actually got done, and
   one that did not. What was different about them?
6. What is the hardest part of being the person running it rather than the
   person fixing it? Have you ever failed to keep those two roles separate?
