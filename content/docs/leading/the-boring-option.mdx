title: Knowing When the Boring Option Is Correct
description: Choosing the well-understood thing on purpose, and recognising the cases where it genuinely is not enough.
tags: [leading, decisions, architecture, operations]
---

What this essay should cover: how you decide that the dull, widely-deployed,
well-understood option is the right one — and how you tell that apart from plain
conservatism, because the boring option is sometimes wrong.

## Questions to answer

1. Describe a case where you chose the boring option over something better on
   paper. What did the paper advantage turn out to be worth?
2. Describe a case where the boring option was wrong. What did it fail to do,
   and how long did it take to find out?
3. What is your actual test for well understood — deployments you have seen,
   people on the team who have run it, the quality of its failure documentation?
4. How do you argue for the boring choice to someone who reads it as a lack of
   ambition? Give the words you have used.
5. Describe a piece of novel technology you did adopt. What made it worth the
   unknowns, and what did you put in place in case you were wrong?
6. Which boring choice on your current system is now the constraint? Would you
   make it again knowing that?
