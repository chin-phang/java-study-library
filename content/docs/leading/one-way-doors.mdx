---
title: One-Way and Two-Way Doors
description: Telling reversible decisions from irreversible ones, and spending your deliberation where it pays.
tags: [leading, decisions, architecture]
---

What this essay should cover: how you tell a decision you can walk back from one
you cannot, why the distinction changes how much process a decision deserves,
and what it costs when you misclassify in either direction.

## Questions to answer

1. Name a one-way door you walked through. What made it irreversible — data,
   contracts with other teams, a public API, a hiring decision — and did you
   know at the time?
2. Name a decision you treated as one-way that turned out to be two-way. What
   did the extra caution cost in time or momentum?
3. What is your actual test for reversibility? Try to state it as something
   another person could apply without you in the room.
4. Describe a case where the reversible option was worse on the merits but you
   took it anyway because it was reversible. Was that right?
5. How do you keep a two-way door two-way — what have you done to preserve the
   ability to back out of a choice, and what did that cost?
6. Who gets to decide a one-way door on your team? Has that ever been contested,
   and how did it resolve?
