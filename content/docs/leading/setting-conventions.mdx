title: Setting and Defending Conventions
description: How a team convention gets chosen, written down, enforced, and eventually retired.
tags: [leading, conventions, code-review]
---

What this essay should cover: how a convention goes from your opinion to the
team's rule — what makes one worth the cost of enforcing, how it gets enforced
without you being the bottleneck, and how you kill one that has outlived its
reason.

## Questions to answer

1. Name a convention you introduced that stuck. What was the concrete failure
   that made you introduce it, and how long did it take before people followed
   it without being asked?
2. Name one you introduced that did **not** stick. Why not — was it wrong, was
   it unenforceable, or did you not make the case well enough?
3. Which of your team's conventions are enforced by a tool, and which by review?
   How did you decide which ones were worth automating, and what did the
   automation cost to build and maintain?
4. Describe a time someone senior to you, or from another team, broke a
   convention deliberately. What did you do, and would you do the same again?
5. Which convention have you retired? What told you it was no longer paying for
   itself, and how did you get the team to stop doing it?
6. When a new joiner asks why the team does something a particular way, where
   does the answer live — a document, a lint rule, or a person? What happens
   when that person leaves?
