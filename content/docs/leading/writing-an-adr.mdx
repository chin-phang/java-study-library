What this essay should cover: what an architecture decision record is actually
for — not the template, but the discipline of writing down the constraints and
the rejected options so a future reader can tell whether the decision has
expired.

## Questions to answer

1. Find an ADR you wrote more than a year ago. Does it still read as correct?
   What has changed that the document does not know about?
2. Which section does the real work — context, options considered, consequences?
   Which one do people actually read later?
3. Describe an ADR that changed a decision while it was being written. What did
   the act of writing force you to notice?
4. Have you ever written an ADR for a decision already made and shipped? Was
   that a useful record or a rationalisation?
5. What triggers an ADR on your team, and what does not? Where is the line
   between a decision worth recording and work that is just work?
6. Describe reading someone else's ADR and overturning the decision. What in the
   document made that possible, or what was missing that made it harder than it
   should have been?
