What this essay should cover: how you arrive at an estimate you are willing to
be held to, what you do when it is rejected, and how you cut scope without
quietly cutting the parts that make the thing work.

## Questions to answer

1. Describe an estimate you got badly wrong. What did you fail to see — an
   unknown, a dependency, a review cycle, an environment?
2. How do you communicate uncertainty in a number without it being read as
   padding? Give the exact words you have used.
3. Describe a time you were pushed to commit to a date you did not believe. What
   did you do, and what happened?
4. Name something you cut to make a deadline that you later had to put back.
   What made it look optional at the time?
5. What do you refuse to cut, ever — tests, migrations, observability,
   rollback? Can you defend that in a room that wants the date?
6. Who is the estimate actually for on your team, and what decision does it
   feed? Has knowing that changed how you give one?
