---
title: By symptom
description: The concept pages entered by the failure you are holding, rather than by subject or by dependency.
---

The sidebar lists the concept pages by **subject**. [The study path](/docs/concepts)
lists them by **dependency**. Neither is how you usually arrive. You arrive
holding a p99 graph that climbed for no reason, a customer charged twice, or an
index the planner will not use — and the subject is the thing you are trying to
work out, not the thing you can look up.

So this page lists them by **symptom**: what you observed, before you know what
caused it.

**The line under each symptom is the point.** It says what the failure looks
like and is not — the wrong diagnosis that costs the afternoon. A symptom that
only listed links would be a worse sidebar. Every entry is drawn from the
opening section of a page, because the pages are written to open on a concrete
failure rather than a definition, so the failures were already there to index.

**Two or three pages per symptom, and the order matters** — the first is the
mechanism most likely to be behind it, the rest are the ones that produce the
same observation by a different route. A `ClassCastException` in a method you
never wrote is a bridge method or two classloaders, and those have nothing in
common except the message you are staring at. That is the kind of thing a
subject index cannot tell you.

**This is the one hand-maintained index in the library.** The sidebar is a list
of real files and the study path is derived from frontmatter, but the symptom
text and its page list are typed by hand, so they can drift. Both directions are
checked below rather than assumed: a slug naming no page renders as a defect,
and every concept page no symptom reaches is listed. Neither block appears
today.
